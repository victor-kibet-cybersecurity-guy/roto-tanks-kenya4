Why 3,000L is a practical middle size

It offers more reserve than compact tanks while remaining easier to accommodate than very large tanks. It suits homes, rentals and small operations with moderate demand.

Estimate reserve duration

Divide 3,000 litres by the site daily consumption. If a property uses 750 litres a day, the theoretical reserve is four days. Use measured consumption where possible.

Compare nearby sizes

The 2,000L option costs less but stores one-third less water. The 4,000L and 5,000L options give more buffer if interruptions are longer or demand varies.

Delivery access

Confirm the tank dimensions and route from the road to the final base. Gates, narrow passages, overhead cables and turning space matter before delivery.

Price confirmation

Treat KSh 14,500 as the supplied catalogue reference and request current price, stock and delivery cost before ordering.

Practical 3,000L planning example

If a household uses about 600 litres daily, a full 3,000L tank represents roughly five theoretical days of storage. If demand rises to 1,000 litres daily, reserve falls to three days. A family choosing between 3,000L and 5,000L should therefore compare the cost of two extra days of reserve against available space and budget.

Where 3,000L is often considered

This capacity sits between compact emergency storage and larger household systems. It is useful where a 1,000L tank runs out too quickly but a 5,000L tank is difficult to place. Rental compounds, small shops and homes with moderate rainwater collection may also compare this size.

Site checklist

Ask for the current footprint before constructing the base. Check gate width and the path to the installation point. Keep drainage away from the base and support connected pipework so it does not pull on the tank fitting.
