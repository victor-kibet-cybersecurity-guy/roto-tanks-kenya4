Best Water Tank Sizes for Kenyan Homes

Choose a tank capacity from household demand, reserve days, water-source reliability and available installation space.

Updated 18 August 2026Roto Tanks Kenya EditorialPeople-first buying guide

Start with daily household use

Record or estimate how much water the household uses for drinking, cooking, washing, cleaning and sanitation. A measured figure from bills or meter readings is better than guessing.

Choose how many reserve days you need

If supply interruptions last one day, a short reserve may be enough. Where supply is less predictable, extra days of storage reduce the risk of running dry.

Match common capacities to use cases

1,000L and 2,000L suit compact backup needs. 3,000L to 6,000L cover many homes. 8,000L to 10,000L suit higher demand or longer reserve periods. Larger tanks support institutions, apartments and commercial sites.

Check space before choosing capacity

Measure the installation area and access route. Confirm the exact external dimensions from the manufacturer before delivery, because capacity does not tell you the full footprint.

Plan for future demand

If occupancy, rental units or water use will grow soon, compare one larger tank against adding a second tank later. Include pump and plumbing implications in the decision.

A simple sizing worksheet

Write down the number of people using the water, the approximate litres used each day and the longest interruption you want to cover. Multiply daily demand by reserve days. If five people together use about 600 litres per day and you want four days of reserve, the starting requirement is 2,400 litres. The next listed size above that figure is 3,000L. If the household regularly hosts visitors or expects growth, comparing 4,000L or 5,000L may be sensible.

Why two households need different tanks

A five-person household with reliable water every day does not need the same reserve as a five-person household that receives water only twice a week. Likewise, a home using stored water only for toilets and cleaning needs less capacity than one using the tank for all domestic demand. The source matters too. A borehole can refill storage on demand, while rainwater harvesting depends on rainfall and roof catchment.

Do not forget the site

A mathematically ideal tank is useless if it cannot pass through the gate or fit on the prepared base. Measure the available area, check the delivery route and request the current external dimensions before construction. For roof or elevated installations, use competent structural advice because stored water creates a large permanent load.
