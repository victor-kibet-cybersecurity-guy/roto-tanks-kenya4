Large-site applications

20,000 litres targets high-demand homes, institutions, schools, farms, commercial compounds and properties requiring substantial reserve capacity.

Calculate daily drawdown

At 2,000 litres per day, the theoretical reserve is ten days. At 5,000 litres per day, it is four days. Use actual consumption data to avoid oversizing or undersizing.

Delivery planning becomes critical

A tank of this scale needs a clear transport route, adequate gate and site access, an agreed offloading area and a prepared support base before delivery.

Compare 16,000L and 24,000L

The supplied catalogue lists 16,000L at KSh 67,500 and 24,000L at KSh 135,000. Compare the cost of additional reserve against the installation space available.

Technical checks

Confirm dimensions, manufacturer installation guidance, fittings, warranty information and any site-specific structural requirements before purchase.

20,000L reserve planning

A large property using 4,000 litres per day has about five theoretical days of storage in 20,000 litres. If daily use is 2,500 litres, reserve stretches to about eight days. For institutions and commercial properties, build the calculation from occupancy, sanitation, cleaning, kitchen or process water and any outdoor demand.

Large-capacity system design

A 20,000L tank often sits within a wider system that includes pumps, level controls, distribution pipework and overflow management. Decide whether water enters by municipal supply, borehole, rainwater or tanker delivery. The refill rate determines how much reserve the tank needs to carry between supply periods.

Base and structural planning

Twenty thousand litres of water has a mass of roughly twenty tonnes before including the tank. Ground-level support therefore needs careful preparation, and any elevated installation demands qualified structural design. Do not use improvised stands for this scale of storage.
