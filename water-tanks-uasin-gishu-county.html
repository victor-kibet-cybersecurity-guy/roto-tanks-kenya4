Water Tanks in Uasin Gishu County

A county-level guide to demand, tank sizes, delivery routes and local town pages across Uasin Gishu.

Rapid urban growth sits within a major farming county. Rental developments, hospitals, schools, dairy farms and grain operations all use water differently, so recorded consumption is the strongest sizing input.

Choose capacity from demand, not county name

Start with the whole property’s daily litres and multiply by the number of backup days required. Add a sensible operational margin, then compare the nearest listed sizes. Five people at 50–100 litres each per day need about 250–500 litres daily before gardening, livestock or business use. Rentals should use occupied units; schools should use attendance and boarding status; farms should separate domestic and agricultural demand.

Homes

Compare 2,000L to 6,000L after counting residents, refill frequency and available footprint.

Rentals and institutions

Compare 8,000L, 10,000L and larger systems using peak occupancy and measured daily demand.

Farms and businesses

Separate livestock, irrigation, process and staff water, then plan the refill source and reserve period.

County delivery planning

Rain-affected farm roads, tree-lined approaches, narrow gates and offloading needs become more important as capacities increase.

Every request should include a map pin, contact person, narrowest gate width, road-surface notes, overhead restrictions, turning space and who will offload. Confirm the selected tank’s diameter and height before dispatch. For large or elevated installations, arrange lifting and structural work separately with qualified providers.

Installation and rainwater considerations

Place an above-ground tank upright on a smooth, level and continuous support that carries the full footprint. One litre of water weighs about one kilogram, so even a 5,000L tank imposes roughly five tonnes of water load before the tank and fittings. Control overflow so water cannot erode the base. Where rainwater harvesting is planned, match roof yield and gutter capacity to the tank rather than selecting capacity alone.
