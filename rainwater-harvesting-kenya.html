Rainwater Harvesting Guide Kenya

Plan roof catchment, gutters, first-flush control, filtration, storage capacity and overflow before buying a rainwater tank.

Estimate how much roof water you can collect

Harvested volume depends on roof area, rainfall and collection efficiency. A simple planning formula multiplies roof area by rainfall depth and an efficiency factor, then converts the result to litres.

Keep the first dirty runoff out

Dust, leaves and bird droppings accumulate on roofs. A first-flush arrangement diverts the earliest runoff before cleaner water enters storage.

Use leaf control and filtration

Gutter guards, screens and appropriate filters reduce debris entering the tank. The level of treatment depends on how the water will be used.

Choose storage from rainfall pattern and demand

A larger roof does not automatically require the largest tank. Compare how quickly the roof fills storage with how quickly the household, farm or business uses the water.

Plan overflow safely

Direct overflow away from foundations and erosion-prone areas. A full tank during heavy rain needs a controlled path for excess water.

Maintain the whole system

Clean gutters, inspect screens, manage first-flush devices and periodically check tank condition. Rainwater quality depends on the entire collection system, not the tank alone.

Example roof-yield calculation

As a planning example, 1 millimetre of rain falling on 1 square metre of roof equals about 1 litre before losses. A 100 square metre roof receiving 20 millimetres of rain therefore receives about 2,000 litres of rainfall. Actual harvested volume is lower because of splash, first flush, gutter losses and imperfect collection. This simple relationship helps compare roof size with tank capacity.

Decide how the harvested water will be used

Water used for garden irrigation has different treatment expectations from water used inside a home. If harvested water will be used for drinking or other potable purposes, appropriate treatment and public-health guidance are essential. The tank alone does not make collected roof water safe to drink.

Dry-season planning

During long dry periods, a large tank can still empty. Compare the volume stored at the end of rainy periods with expected daily use during the dry period. Reducing demand, adding another source or increasing storage are separate strategies, and the best mix depends on the property.
