Temporary traffic control use

Road cones and plastic barriers help mark hazards, work areas and controlled routes. They should form part of a wider traffic-management plan rather than being treated as a complete safety system.

Visibility and placement

Correct spacing, visibility and positioning depend on the site and traffic conditions. Follow applicable road or workplace requirements for the location where the products will be used.

Handling and storage

Plastic traffic products are designed for repeated deployment, but storage and transport should avoid crushing or unnecessary damage. Check the product condition before each use.

Quotation details

For larger quantities, send the product code, quantity and delivery location. Confirm current stock and unit pricing before committing to a site schedule.

Use the right number of devices

A single cone does not create a safe work zone. Site layout, approach speed, visibility and hazard type determine how many cones or barriers are required and where they should sit. Follow the relevant traffic-management or workplace safety plan.

Night and poor-visibility conditions

Visibility requirements change after dark, in rain or in dusty environments. Confirm whether the selected product needs reflective elements, warning lights or additional signage for the intended use.

Bulk project enquiries

For construction sites or events, request a quotation by product code and quantity. Include the delivery town and required date, then confirm current stock before relying on the items in a site programme.
