Water Tanks for Farms in Kenya

Plan farm water storage for livestock, cleaning, borehole pumping, rainwater harvesting and other agricultural needs.

Separate livestock and household demand

Farm storage often serves more than one purpose. Calculate livestock watering, cleaning and domestic use separately before combining the total.

Livestock demand changes with conditions

Animal type, herd size, feed, weather and production stage affect water consumption. Use farm-specific guidance rather than a single fixed figure.

Coordinate storage with the source

A borehole with slow yield needs enough storage to accumulate water between peak-use periods. Rainwater systems need storage matched to roof collection and seasonal rainfall.

Use troughs and distribution wisely

Large central storage can feed cattle troughs or smaller distribution points. Plan valves and pipe routes so maintenance does not drain the whole system unnecessarily.

Prepare for delivery access

Farm sites often have narrow tracks or soft ground. Give clear access information when requesting delivery of large tanks.

Separate critical and optional farm uses

Livestock drinking water is usually more critical than activities that can be postponed, such as some washing or irrigation tasks. During a supply failure, knowing which uses must continue helps determine the reserve volume that truly matters. Consider separate tanks or isolation valves if farm and household demand compete for the same storage.

Use gravity where appropriate

Some farms place storage at a suitable elevation to feed troughs or distribution points by gravity. Any raised tank or platform must be structurally designed for the full water load. A ground tank with a pump is often safer than an improvised elevated stand.

Keep trough systems maintainable

Plan shut-off valves, float controls and drain points so individual troughs can be cleaned or repaired without emptying the main tank. Protect pipework from livestock damage and vehicle movement where lines cross working areas.
