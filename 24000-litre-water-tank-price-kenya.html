When 24,000 litres makes sense

This capacity suits sites with high daily demand, long reserve requirements or a water source that fills storage intermittently.

Storage duration examples

At 3,000 litres per day, 24,000 litres represents eight theoretical days. At 6,000 litres per day, it represents four days. Design around measured site demand.

Plan the site before ordering

Confirm the exact footprint, height, base requirements and vehicle access before committing. Large tanks are difficult to reposition once delivery logistics begin.

Single large tank versus multiple tanks

Some sites prefer one large tank. Others use multiple smaller tanks for redundancy, phased installation or easier placement. Plumbing design and total cost determine the better approach.

Planning the largest listed cylindrical size

The 24,000L tank is not simply a bigger household tank. It changes delivery, site preparation and plumbing requirements. The intended installation area should be selected before purchase, with adequate vehicle approach, offloading space, foundation support and room to connect pipework.

When multiple tanks may be better

Two 12,000L-equivalent storage units are not part of the supplied list, but the general system question still matters: one large tank versus multiple smaller tanks. Multiple tanks give isolation and redundancy, while a single tank reduces the number of vessels and connections. A designer should compare footprints, cost, pump arrangement and maintenance needs.

Water-source matching

Large storage is most valuable when the source can eventually replenish it. A low-yield borehole may fill a large tank slowly over many hours, which can be useful for peak-demand buffering. Rainwater harvesting needs enough roof catchment and rainfall to justify the volume. Tanker-fed sites should compare delivery volume and frequency.
