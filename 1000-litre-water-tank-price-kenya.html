Who a 1,000L tank suits

This compact capacity works as short-term backup, point-of-use storage or a small property tank where water supply is fairly regular.

How long 1,000 litres lasts

Duration depends entirely on consumption. Divide 1,000 by your measured daily use to estimate reserve days. A household using 500 litres a day would get about two days before allowing for unusable residue or changing demand.

Space and placement

The 1,000L size is easier to place than larger tanks, but you should still confirm external dimensions, a level full-support base and access for delivery.

When to move to 2,000L or 3,000L

Choose a larger tank if supply interruptions last longer, household size is growing or the tank also supports outdoor cleaning, rental units or other high-demand uses.

Quotation questions

Ask whether the KSh 6,500 catalogue price is current, whether stock is available and how much delivery costs to your county and town.

Practical 1,000L planning example

Assume a small household uses 350 litres per day from the storage tank. Dividing 1,000 litres by 350 gives a theoretical reserve of slightly under three days. If the same property uses 700 litres daily, the reserve falls to about one and a half days. This shows why capacity should be connected to actual use rather than household size alone.

Advantages of a compact tank

A 1,000L tank is easier to fit into small compounds and easier to transport than large capacities. It also works well as secondary storage, for example where a main tank feeds a smaller point-of-use tank. The trade-off is limited reserve, so frequent outages or high daily demand may justify moving up to 2,000L or 3,000L.

Before delivery

Confirm the exact dimensions, clear the route to the final position and prepare a stable level base. Check whether the outlet fitting and valve you need are included. Send the exact delivery town when requesting the current price so transport is considered at the same time.
