Where a 5,000L tank fits

5,000 litres is a common planning point for homes, rentals, rainwater harvesting, small farms and businesses needing more than short emergency storage.

Estimate how long 5,000 litres lasts

Divide capacity by measured daily use. At 1,000 litres per day, 5,000 litres represents about five theoretical days. At 500 litres per day, it represents about ten days. Actual usable duration varies with demand.

Compare 4,000L, 5,000L and 6,000L

The supplied prices are KSh 18,500 for 4,000L, KSh 24,500 for 5,000L and KSh 27,500 for 6,000L. If your site and budget allow, compare the cost of extra reserve rather than focusing on one size only.

Base and installation space

Confirm the exact footprint before constructing the base. The support should be level, stable and provide full-bottom support according to current manufacturer guidance.

Delivery and fittings

Ask about transport to your town, offloading, outlet fittings and whether additional plumbing work is required. These items affect the final installed cost.

Before you pay

Confirm current stock, the latest price, product dimensions and any warranty or manufacturer documentation relevant to your purchase.

5,000L household reserve examples

A property using 750 litres per day would have about six and a half theoretical days from 5,000 litres. At 1,250 litres per day, the same tank provides about four days. These examples help explain why 5,000L works well for some homes but feels small for a busy apartment compound or institution.

4,000L versus 5,000L versus 6,000L

The jump from 4,000L to 5,000L adds 25 percent more capacity. Moving from 5,000L to 6,000L adds another 20 percent. Compare those capacity gains with the supplied catalogue prices and the physical space required. If outages are infrequent, the smaller option may be adequate. If water is unreliable, the extra reserve may be worth the difference.

Common buying questions

Ask whether the current quotation includes delivery, whether the tank is in stock, what fittings are supplied and what base is recommended. If using the tank for rainwater harvesting, also plan the gutter, screen, first-flush and overflow system. If using it with a borehole, consider pump control and how quickly the source refills storage.
