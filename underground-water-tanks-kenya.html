Underground Water Tanks in Kenya

Plan buried water storage around site conditions, underground-rated products, excavation, drainage and maintenance access.

Use a tank designed for burial

Underground tanks face external soil loads that above-ground tanks do not. Do not bury a normal cylindrical water tank unless the manufacturer explicitly specifies it for underground installation.

Assess soil and water conditions

Groundwater, poor drainage and unstable soil can change the installation method. Obtain site-specific professional advice and the current product installation instructions before excavation.

Excavation and backfilling

The excavation must allow safe placement and the specified bedding or support. Backfill material and sequence should follow manufacturer guidance to avoid uneven loading or deformation.

Keep access for maintenance

Plan inspection access, inlet and outlet points, pumps if used, overflow and the route for future servicing. A buried tank that cannot be inspected becomes harder to maintain.

Compare listed capacities

The supplied underground catalogue includes 2,000L, 5,000L, 10,000L and 16,000L listings. Choose capacity only after confirming the site supports the planned installation.

Groundwater and buoyancy risk

An empty or partly filled underground tank can be affected by surrounding groundwater. Site conditions therefore matter during both installation and operation. Do not assume a dry excavation today will remain dry throughout the year. Ask for the current manufacturer installation method and seek site-specific engineering advice where groundwater is possible.

Protect the tank from surface loads

Parking areas, driveways and other loaded surfaces introduce additional forces. A buried tank should not carry vehicle loads unless the installation design explicitly accounts for them. The access cover and any protective structure should also match the planned surface use.

Maintenance planning

Keep inspection access reachable after landscaping or paving. Plan pumps, sensors, vents, overflow and cleaning access before backfilling. Future service work becomes expensive when the original installation hides every connection beneath permanent finishes.
