Common Water Tank Installation Mistakes

Avoid unstable bases, wrong tank type, poor access planning, unsupported plumbing and unsafe raised platforms.

Building the base before confirming dimensions

A base based on a guessed tank diameter may end up too small. Get the exact dimensions first, then build adequate full-bottom support.

Placing the tank on uneven ground

Stones, ridges and sloping surfaces concentrate load. The filled tank needs a level, stable support consistent with manufacturer guidance.

Burying an above-ground tank

Standard cylindrical tanks are not automatically suitable for burial. Use an underground-rated tank when the installation is below ground.

Ignoring delivery access

Customers sometimes prepare a perfect base but forget the tank must pass through the gate, around buildings and under cables before reaching it.

Letting pipes pull on the outlet

Rigid unsupported plumbing can transfer stress to tank fittings. Support pipework independently and follow correct connection guidance.

Improvised elevated stands

Thousands of litres create huge loads. Raised platforms need proper structural design and construction.

No overflow or drainage plan

Overflow can erode the base or flood nearby structures. Route excess water away safely.

Skipping final inspection

Before filling, inspect support, fittings, valves, access and overflow. Correct small problems while the tank is empty.

Connecting the tank before the base is ready

Plumbing work sometimes starts while the tank is still sitting temporarily on soil or blocks. Once pipes are fixed, moving the tank onto the proper base becomes difficult and fittings may be forced out of alignment. Finish and inspect the support first, place the tank, then connect plumbing.

Ignoring future maintenance space

A tank squeezed tightly between walls can be difficult to inspect, clean or replace. Leave practical access around fittings and the lid. If pumps and filters sit beside the tank, allow enough working room to service them without dismantling the installation.

Failing to secure the water source

Open borehole lines, dirty rainwater gutters or uncovered tanker-fill points can introduce contamination even when the tank itself is clean. Treat the full water path, from source to tank to taps, as one system.
