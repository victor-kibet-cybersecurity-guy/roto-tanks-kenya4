Application sizing guide

Water Tanks for Construction Sites in Kenya

Size temporary or project storage from curing, mixing, cleaning, worker welfare and refill logistics.

Construction water demand changes by phase. Concrete work and curing can create short high-demand periods, while welfare and cleaning continue throughout the project. Coordinate the tank with tanker delivery or mains availability and keep potable supply separate where required.

Capacity ranges to compare

5,000L to 10,000L is a practical starting comparison for smaller sites; 16,000L to 24,000L or linked tanks can support larger works when refill intervals and vehicle access justify the capacity.

Calculate the reserve

  1. Record or estimate the whole site’s litres per day.
  2. Choose the number of backup days or hours the site must operate.
  3. Separate domestic, irrigation, process, livestock or fire water where relevant.
  4. Add a practical margin without exceeding available refill, space or budget.
  5. Use the five-question tank wizard to compare the nearest listed capacities.

Installation and operational risks

Protect the tank from plant movement, falling materials and unstable temporary bases. Define who controls outlets, how water is metered and how the tank will be moved without damage when the site changes.

A litre of stored water weighs about one kilogram. The support for 10,000L therefore carries approximately ten tonnes of water before the tank, fittings and structural platform are added. Ground bases must support the full footprint continuously; elevated structures require professional design.

Quotation checklist

  • Exact capacity, height variant and colour
  • Current tank price and stock date
  • Inlet, outlet, overflow, valves and pump requirements
  • Delivery destination, gate width and offloading plan
  • Base or tower responsibility and installation scope
  • Written warranty and claim process
