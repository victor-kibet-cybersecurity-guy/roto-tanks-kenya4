Why the delivered price differs from the tank price

Transport distance, tank size, vehicle access, offloading and fittings affect the final amount. A large tank delivered to an upcountry site needs different logistics from a small tank collected near Nairobi.

Choosing by budget without undersizing

A cheaper tank can cost more later if it runs dry too quickly. Estimate the reserve volume you need first, then compare prices among sizes that meet that requirement.

What to ask for in a quotation

Request the exact tank size, current unit price, stock status, delivery charge to your town, fittings included, offloading arrangement and any installation requirements.

How to read a tank price list

A price list becomes useful when you compare price per litre, not only the headline amount. For example, moving from a smaller tank to a larger one may increase the purchase price while lowering the cost per litre of stored capacity. That does not automatically make the larger tank the better choice. Extra capacity only has value if you have the space, support base, water source and actual demand to use it. Start with your required storage volume, then compare the prices of two or three nearby sizes.

Budget for the whole installation

Set aside money for more than the tank itself. Depending on the site, you may need a prepared base, valves, pipework, connectors, a pump, overflow plumbing, gutter connections for rainwater harvesting or labour. Delivery is especially important for large capacities because transport and offloading arrangements vary by destination. Ask for an itemised quotation so you understand which items are included and which remain your responsibility.

Price-check checklist

  • Match the quotation to the exact capacity and product name.
  • Confirm whether the quoted amount includes VAT where applicable.
  • Ask whether fittings or accessories are included.
  • Confirm delivery and offloading separately.
  • Keep the written quotation until the order is complete.
