Water Storage for Apartments in Kenya

Plan apartment storage around occupancy, peak demand, reserve days, pumping, roof or ground placement and future growth.

Updated 18 August 2026Roto Tanks Kenya EditorialPeople-first buying guide

  2. Plan for peak periods
  3. Choose reserve days
  4. Ground tank and roof tank roles
  5. Maintenance access
  6. Questions

Count users and units

Start with occupied units and realistic user numbers rather than building size alone. Demand changes as occupancy changes, so include a growth allowance for vacant units that may soon be occupied.

Plan for peak periods

Morning and evening water use can exceed the daily average rate. Pumps, pipe sizes and storage need to handle these peaks without leaving upper floors short of water.

Choose reserve days

Landlords should decide how long tenants need water during municipal or borehole interruptions. Multiply daily building demand by that reserve period.

Ground tank and roof tank roles

Some buildings use ground storage with pumps to higher-level storage. Structural design is essential for any elevated tank because water loads are substantial.

Maintenance access

Place tanks, valves and pumps where they remain serviceable. A system hidden behind permanent construction becomes expensive to repair later.

Example apartment calculation

If a small apartment block uses 4,000 litres per day and the owner wants two full days of reserve, the starting requirement is 8,000 litres. A 10,000L tank provides some buffer. If occupancy is expected to rise, the owner might compare 16,000L or a multi-tank configuration. Use actual meter or purchase records when available.

Pressure and pumping are separate from capacity

A large ground tank does not guarantee good pressure at upper floors. Pump selection, pipe sizing, pressure controls and elevation determine distribution performance. Treat storage volume and delivery pressure as connected but separate engineering questions.

Tenant continuity and maintenance

Multiple tanks or isolation valves can reduce downtime during cleaning or repairs. Plan access so pumps, valves and tanks remain serviceable without entering private units or dismantling permanent building finishes.
